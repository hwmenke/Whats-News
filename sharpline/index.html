How SharpLine prices a game

1 · Power ratings

Every team carries a rating in points relative to a league-average club. The difference between two ratings is the expected margin on a neutral field. Billy Walters' operation lived and died by these numbers — his were rebuilt weekly from play-by-play data and injury intel. Yours are editable on the Power Ratings tab; the model is a tool you steer, not an oracle.

2 · Home field & situations

A baseline home-field advantage (default 2.0 points, configurable) is added, plus situational nudges Walters tracked obsessively: rest edges off a bye, short-week penalties, and long cross-country trips for road teams.

3 · Key numbers

NFL margins cluster on 3 and 7. SharpLine converts its predicted margin into probabilities using a margin distribution re-weighted at the key numbers, so the move from −2.5 to −3 is priced as far more valuable than −7.5 to −8 — exactly the half-points sharp bettors fight for.

4 · De-vigged market consensus

Sportsbook moneylines are stripped of vig with the power method (which puts more of the juice on favourites), then blended with Polymarket and Kalshi prices, which trade near fair probability. The result is the market's true opinion — the number you must beat.

5 · Bet only with an edge, size with Kelly

A play is flagged only when the model's probability beats the break-even price by your threshold (default 2%). Stakes are fractional Kelly (default one-quarter), hard-capped at 3% of bankroll per game — Walters never bet more than a low single-digit share of his roll on one outcome, no matter how good the number looked.

6 · Grade yourself on CLV

Results over a season are mostly noise; closing-line value isn't. The bet tracker records the price you got versus the close. If your average CLV is positive, you are doing the one thing Walters said matters: consistently beating the number the market settles on.

SharpLine is an analytics tool, not betting advice. Bet legally, bet what you can afford, and remember the market is very good.
